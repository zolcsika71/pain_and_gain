## Observations and proposals

1. **Priority 1 — propose healer support positioning.** In replay `6ab867aee03513115f91edc0`, owned `pg_player1_melee_1` falls from 1,600 hits at tick 46 to 1,534 at 47 and 258 at 52. At tick 47 its nearest owned healer is four tiles away; at ticks 48–52 the nearest is four or five tiles away. All three listed healers have six functioning HEAL parts, but none is within the documented ranged-heal distance of three. In replay `6ab9398a22f1123ef118f173`, the same melee ID is at (53,54) with 1,442 hits at tick 113 and 386 at 114; its nearest functioning healer is four tiles away on both ticks, and the creep is absent at 115. Across the available snapshots, a damaged owned creep lacks a functioning healer within three tiles on 21 ticks in the older replay and 11 in the newer one; this count includes late ticks after healer losses. **Inference:** keeping a healer within range of an exposed fighter could create healing opportunities and may improve survival. It does not prove a particular heal would have landed or offset simultaneous damage. Prototype a bounded support objective for healers, retaining explicit role/flag tradeoffs. Verify with code-provenance matches: count damaged fighter-ticks inside heal range, issued heal actions where observable, time to loss, retained flags, and outcomes across multiple opponents.

2. **Priority 2 — propose stabilizing local combat objectives.** In replay `6ab9398a22f1123ef118f173`, `pg_player1_ranged_5` is at (49,48) on tick 113 with six functioning RANGED_ATTACK and six MOVE parts, zero fatigue, and an enemy at (54,53), range five. It is at (50,47) on tick 114, when the nearest enemy is outside the candidate's five-tile engagement window, then returns to (49,48) at tick 115 with an enemy again at range five. On the associated terrain, the only immediately range-reducing neighbor at tick 113, (50,49), is plain but occupied by an owned scout. **Inference:** the staged range-five cutoff would alternate approach and flag fallback on these states; congestion may contribute, but the pathfinder's chosen action and exact running code are unknown. Consider retaining a local target briefly or accounting for reachable paths before switching to the flag. Verify by comparing target-switch and range-three-access counts on the same snapshots, then run a code-provenance active match and inspect actual movement, CPU use, and outcomes.

3. **Priority 3 — evaluate other flag objectives after the first capture.** Both replays select `pg_flag_vulnerability` at (49,49) on every available tick. It is already owned by us by tick 40, yet remains selected through ticks 105 and 139. At tick 100 the older replay has that one flag and six neutral flags; the newer has one owned flag, five neutral flags, and an enemy-owned flag at (85,49). **Inference:** assigning some units to another flag could raise scoring, but taking additional flags applies army-wide debuffs and may weaken the current defense. Compare candidate assignments using both distinct saved terrains, then use repeated active matches to measure flag possession duration, net score, casualties, and outcomes. Do not assume an extra flag is always beneficial.

The current staged combat-positioning change addresses access to nearby enemies and is **in progress**, not a completed implementation of these three proposals. None of the replay comparisons establishes improved match outcomes. The present review can finish based on the available evidence; completing it does not waive the live check required before committing that separate movement candidate.
