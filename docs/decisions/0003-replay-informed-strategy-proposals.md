# ADR 0003: Evaluate replay-informed strategy proposals

Status: Proposed strategy evaluation. The original combat-positioning and narrow engagement-boundary changes were committed separately; range-six retention was not exercised live, and the user accepted that residual uncertainty for the checkpoint. A focused reactive healer-positioning rule is implemented and its approach into healing range was observed in a build-identified replay. A bounded, disabled-by-default one-scout flag-allocation experiment and a separate pre-injury one-healer escort experiment are implemented. The healer escort has passed synthetic readiness and is locally activated for a later user-launched validation match; live behavior and strategic benefit remain unverified.

The flag-allocation implementation is a disabled experimental checkpoint. Replay `6ab9a6bd6fdfd16d98709adc` identified the enabled build and showed no available-console errors, but did not confirm an allocation command, its target, or a strategic benefit. Its enabled live-behavior verdict remains inconclusive; the user accepted that limitation for this checkpoint.

## Context

Four validated response chunks from replays `6ab867aee03513115f91edc0` (ticks 1–105) and `6ab9398a22f1123ef118f173` (ticks 1–139) were reviewed with their explicitly associated, checksum-validated maps. ## Proposed evaluation order

1. **Healer support positioning — reactive rule implemented; pre-injury escort prepared for activation, benefit unverified.** Damaged `pg_player1_melee_1` is four or more tiles from every functioning healer at older-replay ticks 47–52 and newer-replay ticks 113–114, outside ranged-heal distance three. A build-identified later replay has an injured `pg_player1_melee_4` four or five tiles from all three pure healers at tick 46. The reactive rule makes pure healers approach the nearest living damaged ally at range four or five, hold when one is within three and none is farther within five, and otherwise use the first flag. The separate escort experiment may retain one healthy pure healer with one healthy engaging pure melee ally for up to 12 ticks, holding within range two and approaching at range three to five. It was introduced disabled and is now enabled for a later validation match. Any friendly injury immediately returns it to reactive movement. Mixed-role creeps retain combat movement; healing-action selection is unchanged. Replay `6aba85516fdfd126c670b262` motivates this experiment but does not prove earlier movement would succeed or change survival. Test support-range availability and friendly survival without unacceptable healer exposure or flag loss; local selector tests and counterfactual objectives alone are insufficient.
2. **Stable local combat targets — narrow rule implemented.** Newer-replay `pg_player1_ranged_5` moves between (49,48), (50,47), and (49,48) at ticks 113–115 while enemy range crosses the five-tile cutoff. The committed rule keeps the previous living target for one tick at range six only when no enemy is within five; range seven, a second outside tick, target loss, or weapon loss releases it. Normal nearest-target selection inside five tiles remains unchanged. This can remove one approach/flag/approach decision cycle in a same-target range-5 → 6 → 5 scenario, but the old capture is no longer available for full re-evaluation, congestion may explain the observed step, and issued commands were not logged. Compare actual range-three access, target switches, CPU cost, and outcomes in new code-provenance matches before claiming a benefit.
3. **Post-capture flag allocation — optional experiment, default off.** At tick 100 both earlier replays still select an already owned first flag while other flags are neutral or enemy-owned. Build-identified replay `6ab99d906fdfd159c8709976` independently shows first-flag selection through tick 159 after ownership at tick 39, with six other flags never ours. The local experiment may send one full-health MOVE-only scout to a neutral attack-reduction flag only with a nearby combat defender, no local threat, a complete bounded route, and a five-step estimated lead over enemies. It ends on capture, danger, invalidation, or timeout, without chaining; combat and healer movement remain unchanged. A captured attack-reduction flag adds three points per tick but imposes a 20% army-wide melee penalty while held, even after the scout returns. Compare enabled and disabled builds on flag uptime, net score, first-flag retention, casualties, and outcomes across matches; capture alone is not improvement. [Policy and limitations](../architecture/combat-positioning-validation.md#one-scout-flag-allocation-experiment--2026-09-28).

## Consequences

These are evidence-backed hypotheses, not established strategic improvements. Their order reflects the immediate observed loss of heal access, then possible movement switching, then a scoring tradeoff. The pure-healer rule is implemented, not a verified strategic improvement. Replay `6ab995f16fdfd118847097eb` identifies the prior build and shows a single tick of healer separation. New build-identified replay `6ab99d906fdfd159c8709976` shows a pure healer move from range five to range two of an injured ally over ticks 117–120, while moving farther from the first flag; this supports live execution of the positioning rule but does not log commands or attribute a healing action. The user's earlier checkpoint acceptance of unexercised range-six retention did not establish strategic benefit or waive validation for this new candidate.
